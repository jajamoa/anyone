The Wrong Person Scores the Same: User-Simulator Metrics Never Checked Whose Response They Reward

Chance Jiajie Li1

1MIT Media Lab

jiajie@mit.edu

Finding Free-text user simulators (HumanLM, Turing-RL) are trained and scored by an LLM judge comparing the generated reply to the person’s real one. Feed the model the wrong person and the score does not move. A structured probe of the person’s reasoning drops from 50 to chance.

Why People on the same post agree on the verdict and differ in the reason. Similarity scores the verdict. Identity is in the warrant.

Paper (soon) Code BibTeX

Six people in, one voice out. Whichever history goes in, the similarity judge scores the white beam the same.

The missing control

Nobody asked whether the score is about the person.

The judge score is both the RL reward and the evaluation in HumanLM (Wu et al. 2026) and Turing-RL (Wang et al. 2026). The control: swap the target’s history for another commenter’s on the same post and score again. If the metric measures the person, the score drops. Neither paper runs it; none of 30 surveyed papers on free-text simulation does.

Design

Same post, three conditions, three rulers.

SAME
Generate with the target’s own comment history.
CROSS
Generate with another commenter’s history on the same post. Wrong person, same post.
SAME2
Generate again with the target’s history. This is the noise floor.

Person effect = same − cross; noise floor = same − same2; user-clustered bootstrap. Each text is scored by HumanLM’s judge, embedding cosine, and the SUITE probe (stance NTA / YTA; warrant, one of ten moral principles), against labels from the real comment.

No ruler can find a person the text does not contain. Table A used Sonnet. The pilot is being regenerated with Sonnet so all three rulers meet on the same texts.

C. The probe reads structure off real text

Given only the real comment, a reader model recovers the labels at 98.3% (stance) and 65.0% (warrant; human annotators 73%). The probe applies to any simulator’s free text.

Posts with three or more commenters. Similarity covers the verdict, the facts and the genre, which everyone shares. What differs between people is one clause.

Proposal

Score the structure.

Agent answers the probe

METHOD 1

The simulator gets the context and the stance / warrant questions built from the real comment. This is SUITE; gives Table A.

Read the probe off the text

METHOD 2

The simulator generates freely; a reader answers the same questions from the text alone. Works on HumanLM and Turing-RL outputs unchanged.

A fidelity signal should move when the person changes and hold when only the sample changes. Warrant passes by fifty to one. Similarity fails, and similarity is the reward.
